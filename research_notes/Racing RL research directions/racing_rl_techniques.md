# Zero-Shot Cross-Track Generalization in Autonomous-Racing RL: Techniques Beyond the Baseline Plan

Scope note: the assigning team's baseline already covers dynamic-bicycle/Pacejka physics, spline-based procedural track generation, PPO/SAC/TD3 (SB3), domain randomization of curvature/width, and DreamerV3-torch evaluated zero-shot vs. held-out tracks and a min-curvature/MPC reference. This note deliberately excludes re-describing that plan and focuses on what else the literature offers, weighted toward 2022–2026 work. Where a source is foundational/pre-2022 it is flagged explicitly.

## Q1: Model-based RL / world-model approaches beyond Dreamer (TD-MPC/TD-MPC2, MuZero-style, other latent-dynamics agents)

### Takeaway
Beyond Dreamer, the two other model-based families with concrete racing-adjacent evidence are (a) TD-MPC/TD-MPC2-style implicit world models, which appear in autonomous-racing benchmarks but with an important caveat about zero-shot capability, and (b) MuZero-adjacent latent-planning approaches, best evidenced by a Learn-to-Race-challenge-winning VAE + nearest-neighbor latent planner (not literally MuZero, but in the same "plan in a learned latent space" family). A 2025 "context-aware model-based RL" paper explicitly tries to fuse context/meta-RL inference with a Dreamer-style world model for racing generalization, benchmarking against Dreamer, PPO, SAC, and TD-MPC.

### Cited Findings
- TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., original paper) is a scalable, robust implicit world-model algorithm for continuous control; a table of "TD-MPC2 hyperparameters" used "across all tracks and cars" appears in autonomous-racing-adjacent material, indicating it has been applied/benchmarked in multi-track racing settings — but the specific paper/venue for that racing application could not be confirmed beyond a ResearchGate figure caption. — [TD-MPC2 hyperparameters figure](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/TD-MPC2-hyperparameters-We-use-the-same-hyperparameters-across-all-tracks-and-cars_tbl3_382492553); [TD-MPC2 paper](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2310.16828) (single source, not independently verified for the racing-specific claim)
- A follow-up, "TD-M(PC)2: Improving Temporal Difference MPC Through Policy Constraint," addresses stability issues in TD-MPC2 via a policy-constraint mechanism (2025). — [arXiv:2502.03550](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03550v1) (found in one search pass; not independently cross-verified for racing application)
- A WebSearch-tool synthesis (not a primary source) characterized TD-MPC2 as requiring "training on all target tasks simultaneously rather than enabling zero-shot generalization to completely unseen tasks or embodiments" — i.e., the base algorithm as published is not inherently a zero-shot-generalization method; this is an important caveat for the team if they consider swapping in TD-MPC2 as a Dreamer alternative. — synthesized characterization, not a direct paper quote; treat as low-confidence and verify against the original TD-MPC2 paper before relying on it.
- "Context-Aware Model-Based Reinforcement Learning for Autonomous Racing" (Emran Yasser Moustafa and Ivana Dusparic, arXiv:2510.11501, Oct. 2025) integrates context inference (meta-RL-style) with a Dreamer-style latent world model so the policy can condition on inferred track/friction/weather context and adapt zero-shot to held-out tracks; it benchmarks against Dreamer, PPO, SAC, and TD-MPC. Exact numeric results were not extractable from the fetched PDF text (tables/figures were not machine-readable in the fetch) — this is a **gap**, but the method description and baseline set were readable. — [arXiv:2510.11501](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11501)
- "Solving Learn-to-Race Autonomous Racing Challenge by Planning in Latent Space" (Beohar, Heinrich, Kala, Ritter, Melnik; SL4AD Workshop, ICML 2022; arXiv:2207.01275) is a MuZero-adjacent latent-planning approach: U-Net road segmentation → VAE encoding of the road mask into a latent space → nearest-neighbor search over latent states to pick actions (not a learned dynamics/value model in the strict MuZero sense, but "plan by search in a learned latent space"). Team "UniTeam" was among the final winners of the Learn-to-Race Single Camera track. Reported results: ~105 km/h average speed on the known track (Stage 1), ~73 km/h on the unknown/held-out track (Stage 2), zero off-road violations. — [arXiv:2207.01275](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.01275)
- True MuZero-style (learned dynamics + value + policy with MCTS) applications specifically to racing/driving were not found; the closest hits were generic MuZero-interpretability work ("Demystifying MuZero Planning," arXiv:2411.04580) and "EMTS" (expert-guided motion-encoding tree search, arXiv:2309.15079), which extends a MuZero-like tree search with a motion-primitive latent space and expert-policy guidance for efficiency, but this appears aimed at general planning/motion tasks rather than being validated as a racing zero-shot generalization benchmark — could not confirm racing-specific results from a single search snippet. — [arXiv:2411.04580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04580); [arXiv:2309.15079](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.15079) (unverified beyond title/abstract-level search snippet)
- "InDRiVE: Reward-Free World-Model Pretraining for Autonomous Driving" (Khanzada and Kwon, arXiv:2512.18850, Dec. 2025) is a DreamerV3-style agent but with a reward-free intrinsic-motivation twist: it uses **latent ensemble disagreement** (epistemic-uncertainty proxy) to drive exploration during world-model pretraining in CARLA, then does zero-shot or few-shot fine-tuning on downstream lane-following/collision-avoidance tasks in unseen towns/routes. Claims stronger zero-shot robustness and better collision avoidance under town distribution shift than baselines at matched interaction budgets (exact numbers not extracted). This is a CARLA (full driving sim) result, not F1TENTH/MicroRacer/CarRacing, so relevance to lightweight racing sims should be treated as analogous rather than direct. — [arXiv:2512.18850](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.18850)
- Foundational/background (pre-2022, not "latest" but the direct precedent the team's Dreamer choice already builds on): Brunnbauer et al., "Latent Imagination Facilitates Zero-Shot Transfer in Autonomous Racing" (arXiv:2103.04909) — the MicroRacer-adjacent Dreamer paper explicitly named in the assignment as a lineage source. — [arXiv:2103.04909](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.04909)

### Inferences
- The field has not converged on a single "Dreamer replacement" for racing zero-shot generalization; TD-MPC2 and MuZero-style planners are used more as general continuous-control/planning baselines than as generalization-specialized methods in their own right. The 2025 "context-aware model-based RL" paper (Q1 above) suggests the more promising direction is not swapping Dreamer for TD-MPC2/MuZero outright, but augmenting a Dreamer-style world model with an explicit context/task-inference head (bridging into meta-RL, see Q2) — this is the most direct "beyond-Dreamer-but-still-latent-dynamics" technique found for this specific problem.
- Reward-free/intrinsic-motivation world-model pretraining (InDRiVE) is a plausible ablation for the team's Dreamer agent: pretrain the world model with disagreement-driven exploration across randomized tracks before task-reward fine-tuning, which is a cheap addition to a DreamerV3-torch pipeline already in the baseline plan.

- No confirmed application of literal MuZero (learned dynamics+value+policy+MCTS) to F1TENTH/MicroRacer/CarRacing-style zero-shot track generalization was found; this may be a genuine gap in the literature rather than a search failure, since MCTS-style planning is expensive for continuous racing action spaces.

## Q2: Meta-RL and few-shot adaptation for track/environment generalization

### Takeaway
Generic context-based meta-RL (PEARL, RL², and the newer "RL³" hybrid) is well established outside racing, but direct racing applications are thin; the most concrete racing-specific meta-RL result found is a described (not fully verified) meta-RL framework that parameterizes track conditions (e.g., slipperiness/weather) as latent context and meta-validates on unseen tracks. Two more concrete and better-sourced racing adaptation techniques are continual-RL (sequential multi-track pretraining + fine-tuning on a held-out track) and residual-RL-on-classical-controller adaptation (RLPP), both aimed at the same "few-shot on a new track" goal via non-classical-meta-RL machinery.

### Cited Findings
- PEARL (Rakelly et al., "Efficient Off-Policy Meta-RL via Probabilistic Context Variables," ICML 2019 — **foundational/pre-2022**) encodes task information into a latent context variable z inferred from transition context c=(o,a,o′,r); RL² embeds task info into the agent's recurrent memory instead of an explicit latent variable. — [PEARL paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/rakelly19a/rakelly19a.pdf); [BAIR blog on PEARL](https://bair.berkeley.edu/blog/2019/06/10/pearl/)
- "RL³: Boosting Meta Reinforcement Learning via RL Inside RL²" (arXiv:2306.15909) proposes combining traditional per-task RL value estimates as additional input to an RL²-style recurrent meta-learner, intended to generalize better to out-of-distribution tasks than pure RL² — this is a general meta-RL advance (not racing-specific) but directly relevant to the "few-shot domain identification" key question. Racing application not found/confirmed. — [arXiv:2306.15909](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.15909v5)
- A WebSearch-tool synthesis described a "meta-RL framework for racing with varying weather conditions" where the track's "slipperiness" is treated as a latent MDP parameter, the agent starts from meta-knowledge, fine-tunes on particular tracks, and a meta-optimizer updates the meta-knowledge while "meta-validation" evaluates on unseen tracks. **This was not traced to a specific, citable paper/title/venue in the fetched sources** — treat as an unverified claim/possible aggregator paraphrase, not a confirmed fact. Flagged explicitly as a gap below.
- "Enhancing generalization in autonomous driving through track-agnostic reinforcement learning" (Neural Computing and Applications / Springer, 2025, DOI 10.1007/s00521-025-11597-5) extends DRL to racing "without racetrack information," aiming for track-agnostic generalization — full text was behind a Springer auth-wall redirect and could not be fetched; only the search-snippet abstract characterization is available. Authors not confirmed. — [Springer article page](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00521-025-11597-5) (unverified beyond snippet; access blocked)
- "Continual-RL for Generalization in Autonomous Racing on the RoboRacer Platform" (Siegert, Ghignone, Magno; arXiv:2607.24320, 2026) is a continual-learning (not classical meta-RL) approach to the same problem: an async SAC policy is pretrained sequentially across four physical tracks, then fine-tuned on a fifth, held-out track, explicitly evaluating both zero-shot (no adaptation) and few-shot (fine-tuned) performance on real RoboRacer/F1TENTH hardware. Numeric results were not extractable from the PDF fetch (binary/compressed stream issue) — a genuine gap; only the method design is confirmed. — [arXiv:2607.24320](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24320)
- RLPP ("A Residual Method for Zero-Shot Real-World Autonomous Racing on Scaled Platforms," Ghignone, Baumann, Hu, Wang, Xie, Carron, Magno; accepted ICRA 2025; arXiv:2501.17311) is not classical meta-RL but is a concrete, well-sourced few-shot/zero-shot adaptation mechanism: an RL residual is layered on top of a Pure Pursuit controller. Reported results: lap times improved up to 6.37% vs. baseline controllers; closed the gap to state-of-the-art by >52%; achieved reliable zero-shot real-world deployment on F1TENTH; reduced the sim-to-real performance gap by >8x versus a baseline (non-residual) RL policy. Code open-sourced at github.com/forzaeth/rlpp. — [arXiv:2501.17311](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.17311)
- Context inference for generalization also appears fused directly into the world model rather than as a separate meta-RL module in "Context-Aware Model-Based RL for Autonomous Racing" (arXiv:2510.11501, see Q1) — the paper explicitly frames its context-inference component as "meta-reinforcement learning principles."

### Inferences
- Classical context-based meta-RL (PEARL/RL²) does not appear to have a well-documented, directly-citable racing-specific implementation in 2023–2026 literature (as opposed to being cited as generic background); the field has instead moved toward (a) fusing context inference into world models (Q1's arXiv:2510.11501) and (b) engineering-pragmatic alternatives — continual learning and residual RL on a classical controller — that achieve similar "adapt quickly to a new track" goals without the sample-inefficiency meta-RL is known for. For a small team, RLPP-style residual RL on top of a classical controller (e.g., their MPC reference) or the continual-RL sequential-pretraining recipe are likely more tractable near-term experiments than implementing PEARL/RL² from scratch.

- Could not confirm a specific, citable paper for the "meta-RL with track slipperiness as latent context, meta-validated on unseen tracks" framework described in one search synthesis; this may reference a real paper the search snippet paraphrased without preserving the title, or may be a generic explainer conflating techniques. Recommend the report-writer treat this as unconfirmed.
- Could not access full text/results of the Springer "track-agnostic RL" paper (paywalled) or the numeric results of the RoboRacer continual-RL paper (PDF parsing failure) — both would need re-fetching via an ar5iv/HTML mirror or authenticated access for hard numbers.

## Q3: Curriculum learning / procedural-content-generation-for-generalization (UED, PAIRED, minimax regret) applied to driving/racing

### Takeaway
Unsupervised Environment Design (UED) research has a direct, repeatedly-cited racing benchmark lineage: PAIRED's original CarRacing environment, followed by CLUTR (2022/2023) which reports large zero-shot generalization gains over PAIRED specifically on CarRacing, and 2025-era generative/adaptive curriculum work (Bayesian-optimization curricula for racing robustness; a drone-racing "environment as policy" generative curriculum with 7.4x reported generalization improvement) that is the most recent and best-quantified curriculum-for-racing-generalization evidence available.

### Cited Findings
- PAIRED (Dennis et al., NeurIPS 2020 — **foundational/pre-2022**) is the original UED/minimax-regret adversarial curriculum method, validated partly on "a continuous-control car racing environment," producing more robust agents than domain randomization alone. — described via search synthesis, original paper not independently re-fetched in this pass (well-established prior art, low risk of misattribution).
- CLUTR ("Curriculum Learning via Unsupervised Task Representation Learning," Azad, Gur, Emhoff, Alexis, Faust, Abbeel, Stoica; arXiv:2210.10243, submitted Oct. 2022, revised Mar. 2023) decouples task representation from curriculum: (1) trains a recurrent VAE on randomly generated tasks to learn a latent task manifold, (2) trains a teacher to generate curricula by maximizing minimax regret over sampled latent tasks. Tested on **CarRacing** and navigation environments (no F1-specific benchmark confirmed in the abstract, contradicting one earlier search-tool synthesis that mentioned "F1 racing benchmarks" — that synthesis is likely inaccurate/conflated and should not be trusted). Headline result: **10.6x improvement in zero-shot generalization on CarRacing over PAIRED** (45% improvement reported for the navigation task), and CLUTR performs comparably to non-UED SOTA on CarRacing while using **500x fewer environment interactions**. — [arXiv:2210.10243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10243)
- "Improving Environment Robustness of Deep RL Approaches for Autonomous Racing Using Bayesian Optimization-based Curriculum Learning" (Banerjee, Ray, Campbell, Cornell; arXiv:2312.10557, Dec. 2023) models curriculum-parameter-to-performance relationships with a Gaussian Process and selects curricula via an Upper-Confidence-Bound acquisition function, training PPO on a modified CarRacing environment with obstacle avoidance across increasing difficulty tiers. Reported results: BO-generated curriculum achieved 696±113 average reward on difficult test environments vs. 368±181 for default PPO training; obstacle collisions dropped to 1.088 vs. 1.514 for untrained/default agents; BO curricula outperformed manual curricula across all five tested difficulty tiers; training on intermediate difficulty before hard environments produced better transfer than harder-first ordering. — [arXiv:2312.10557](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.10557)
- "Scaling Curriculum Learning For Autonomous Driving" (arXiv:2608.22549, 2026) — introduces CL4AD, described as "the first integration of curriculum learning into batched autonomous driving simulators," framing scenario selection explicitly as a UED problem with utility functions shaping curricula based on success rate and behavior realism alongside standard regret-estimation functions. This is a full autonomous-driving-simulator context (not lightweight racing-specific), and detailed results were not fetched/verified beyond the search-snippet abstract. — [arXiv:2608.22549](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22549) (unverified beyond snippet)
- "Environment as Policy: Generative Curriculum Learning for Autonomous Racing" (Xing, Wang, Messikommer, Scaramuzza; ICLR 2025 Workshop, WRL track) — despite the "autonomous racing" title this is a **drone racing** paper: a secondary RL policy generates/adapts track configurations to balance challenge vs. achievability for the primary racing policy, avoiding the failure mode where training directly on a diverse track set "overburdens the agent." Claims successful zero-shot flight on complex, unseen tracks, outperforming prior environment-shaping techniques, validated in both sim and real world — but the workshop abstract provided no numeric benchmarks. — [mlanthology entry](https://mlanthology.org/iclrw/2025/xing2025iclrw-environment/)
- A closely related, better-quantified drone-racing follow-up: "Bridging Performance and Generalization in Reinforcement Learning for Agile Flight" (Green, Xing, Messikommer, Romero, Scaramuzza; arXiv:2606.27348, submitted June 2026) combines (a) task-aware switching using learning-progress metrics with (b) a physically-informed procedural track generator, explicitly to avoid the failure mode where "existing RL-based racing policies... crash immediately in unseen configurations." Reported headline result: **7.4x improvement in generalization over state-of-the-art approaches while maintaining competitive racing speeds**, validated zero-shot on unseen real-world tracks including vision-based end-to-end control. This appears to be the same Scaramuzza-lab research line as the ICLR-workshop paper above, likely an extended/follow-on version — the exact relationship between the two papers (workshop precursor vs. separate work) could not be fully confirmed from the fetched abstracts. — [arXiv:2606.27348](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27348v1)
- Curriculum learning also appears combined with the offline/behavioral side in "Self-Paced Curriculum Reinforcement Learning for Autonomous Superbike Racing in Simulation" (arXiv:2606.09236) and "Diverse and Adaptive Behavior Curriculum for Autonomous Driving: A Student-Teacher Framework with Multi-Agent RL" (arXiv:2507.19146) — both surfaced in search but not fetched/verified in this pass; flagged as leads for further reading rather than confirmed findings. — [arXiv:2606.09236](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.09236); [arXiv:2507.19146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.19146) (unverified, title/abstract-level only)

### Inferences
- CarRacing (the CoinRun/Procgen-adjacent lightweight benchmark closest to the team's own environment) is the single most common testbed in this literature for UED/curriculum-for-generalization claims (PAIRED, CLUTR), which makes those results the most directly transferable evidence for the team's own spline-track generator — a PAIRED/CLUTR-style adversarial or regret-based curriculum over the team's procedural track parameters (curvature, width, corner radius distributions) is a well-precedented technique the current plan does not use (it uses domain randomization, not adversarial/regret-driven curriculum).
- The drone-racing "environment as policy" / task-aware-switching line (Scaramuzza lab) is the most recent (2025–2026) and best-quantified curriculum-generalization result in a racing-adjacent domain, and its "physically-informed procedural track generator" plus "learning-progress-based task switching" combo is conceptually close to what the team could bolt onto their existing spline-based track generator as an adaptive (rather than static/random) curriculum.

This contradiction between sources is noted explicitly per instructions — trust the abstract-level confirmation (CarRacing/navigation), not the unconfirmed synthesis.
- Could not obtain quantitative results for "Environment as Policy" (ICLR workshop paper) or for CL4AD (arXiv:2608.22549); both are leads with strong claims but only qualitative support in what was fetched.
- Did not verify whether any of these curriculum/UED techniques have been applied to F1TENTH or MicroRacer specifically (as opposed to CarRacing or drone racing) — this looks like a genuine open gap: UED/regret-based curricula for car-racing generalization exist, but not yet demonstrably validated on F1TENTH-style scaled-vehicle tracks in the sources found.

## Q4: Transformers and sequence models in recent racing/driving RL

### Takeaway
Transformers show up in racing RL along three distinct axes: (1) offline/sequence-modeling policies (Decision-Transformer-style and diffusion-policy variants benchmarked on F1TENTH), (2) transformer-based scene/state representation encoders feeding a conventional RL algorithm (SAC), and (3) hybrid imitation approaches that pair a Behavior Transformer with an online adversarial-imitation residual specifically to fix the sequence model's weakness at handling distribution shift / new tracks — this third strand is the most directly relevant to zero-shot generalization.

### Cited Findings
- "F1tenth Autonomous Racing With Offline Reinforcement Learning Methods" (Koirala and Fleming, arXiv:2408.04198, submitted Aug. 2024) reports that online RL agents "consistently fail to complete laps" on the Austria F1TENTH track, motivating a pivot to offline learning from a waypoint-based suboptimal-controller demonstration dataset. It compares **return-conditioned decision-tree-based policies** (their novel contribution) against fully-connected networks, **Transformers**, and **Diffusion Policies**, explicitly examining "zero-shot transferability from seen to unseen scenarios" and adaptation to changed environment dynamics. Full comparative numeric results were not extractable from the fetch (abstract-level detail only) — a gap. — [arXiv:2408.04198](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.04198) / [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04198)
- "BeTAIL: Behavior Transformer Adversarial Imitation Learning from Human Racing Gameplay" (Weaver, Tang, Hao, Kawamoto, Tomizuka, Zhan; arXiv:2402.14194, Feb. 2024, revised Jul. 2024) combines a Behavior Transformer (BeT) policy with an online Adversarial Imitation Learning (AIL) **residual** policy specifically to "correct for out-of-distribution states or shifts in environment dynamics" that pure sequence-modeling struggles with. Trained on Gran Turismo Sport human expert demonstrations across three challenges; the residual approach "reduces environment interactions and improves racing performance and stability, even when the BeT is pretrained on different tracks than downstream learning" — i.e., explicit cross-track transfer evidence. Numeric lap-time/success-rate figures were not extracted in this pass. — [arXiv:2402.14194](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14194v2)
- "Augmenting Reinforcement Learning with Transformer-based Scene Representation Learning for Decision-making of Autonomous Driving" (arXiv:2208.12263) proposes a Scene-Rep Transformer: a multistage transformer encoder for vehicle-interaction awareness plus a sequential latent transformer with self-supervised learning to distill predictive future information, feeding downstream RL decision-making — this is a driving-interaction paper (not racing/track-generalization specific); found via search only, not independently fetched/verified beyond title+snippet. — [arXiv:2208.12263](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.12263) (unverified beyond snippet)
- "Deep reinforcement learning navigation via decision transformer in autonomous driving" (Frontiers in Neurorobotics, 2024, PMC10985319) builds a SAC-based agent that uses a Transformer to model temporal dependencies in observations/actions for partially-observable urban driving — again a driving/navigation, not racing-generalization, application. — [Frontiers article](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurorobotics/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2024.1338189/full) (unverified beyond snippet)
- "DODT: Enhanced Online Decision Transformer Learning through Dreamer's Actor-Critic Trajectory Forecasting" (arXiv:2410.11359) is notable as a direct fusion of Decision-Transformer-style sequence modeling with a Dreamer-style actor-critic for trajectory forecasting, which is conceptually close to what the team's project might explore as a Dreamer/DT hybrid — but it was only found via a search snippet title, not fetched, and its application domain (whether racing-specific) is unconfirmed. — [arXiv:2410.11359](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.11359) (unverified; domain not confirmed)

### Inferences
- The clearest, best-evidenced lesson for the team's generalization goal (rather than raw performance) is BeTAIL's core idea: pure sequence-modeling (Decision-Transformer-style) policies are good at capturing motion style from demonstrations but are fragile to distribution shift/new tracks, and adding an online adversarial-imitation (or plausibly any online RL) residual specifically patches that fragility. This generalizes naturally to a hybrid "offline-pretrained sequence model + online RL residual" recipe the team could test against pure PPO/SAC/TD3 or pure Dreamer.
- Transformers in this literature are used more as **representation-learning backbones** (scene/context encoders feeding a standard RL algorithm) than as end-to-end sequence-modeling controllers for zero-shot track generalization specifically — the strongest "transformer for generalization" evidence is representational, not architectural-replacement-for-Dreamer/PPO.

### Gaps
- Could not extract concrete lap-time/success-rate numbers from either the F1TENTH offline-RL Transformer paper or BeTAIL in this research pass — both would benefit from a direct read of the full PDF/HTML by the team.
- Could not confirm whether DODT (arXiv:2410.11359) is racing-specific; flagged as a promising but unverified lead only.
- No transformer-based *world model* (e.g., a "TransDreamer"/"IRIS"-style transformer world model) applied specifically to racing/F1TENTH/CarRacing was found in this pass — this may be worth a dedicated follow-up search (queries like "transformer world model IRIS racing" or "TransDreamer racing") if the team is specifically interested in that architecture family, since it was not covered here within the tool-call budget.

## Q5: Offline RL and imitation-learning-plus-RL hybrids to bootstrap generalization

### Takeaway
The strongest, most directly on-topic result here is the F1TENTH offline-RL paper (Q4/Q6 overlap) that explicitly builds its offline dataset from a **waypoint-based suboptimal controller** rather than a full MPC/minimum-curvature optimum — a cheaper, more accessible demonstration source than the team's own classical MPC reference, but conceptually validating the "use a classical controller's rollouts as offline-RL demonstrations" approach the team's PROBLEM/IMPLEMENTATION plan could adopt directly (i.e., generate offline data from their own MPC/min-curvature reference and pretrain via CQL/IQL/decision-transformer before or alongside online SAC/PPO/TD3/Dreamer). BeTAIL (Q4) is the second concrete data point, showing a hybrid offline-sequence-model + online-imitation-residual improves cross-track robustness over either alone.
### Cited Findings
- Koirala and Fleming (arXiv:2408.04198, 2024): after online RL (on F1TENTH's Austria track) "consistently fail[ed] to complete laps," the authors built an offline dataset from a **waypoint-based suboptimal controller**, then trained/compared decision-tree-based, fully-connected, Transformer, and Diffusion-Policy offline agents on it, explicitly testing zero-shot transfer to unseen scenarios and adaptation to changed dynamics. This is direct evidence that offline RL bootstrapped from a non-optimal classical controller can succeed where online RL from scratch failed to even complete a lap on a specific track. — [arXiv:2408.04198](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.04198)
- BeTAIL (arXiv:2402.14194, 2024): offline Behavior-Transformer pretraining on **human** Gran Turismo Sport demonstrations, plus an online AIL residual, explicitly improves stability/performance "even when the BeT is pretrained on different tracks than downstream learning" (i.e., cross-track offline-to-online transfer). — [arXiv:2402.14194](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14194v2)
- RLPP (arXiv:2501.17311, 2025, Q2): while not "offline RL" in the dataset sense, it is architecturally the same idea one level up the abstraction — RL as a residual correction on top of a classical controller (Pure Pursuit) rather than a full replacement — and is the best-quantified result in this catalogue (lap time +6.37%, gap-to-SOTA closed >52%, sim-to-real gap reduced >8x). This validates "classical controller + RL residual" more generally as a robust pattern for both offline-demonstration-bootstrapping and online residual correction. — [arXiv:2501.17311](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.17311)
- A "F1TENTH Autonomous Racing With Offline Reinforcement Learning Methods" result (same paper as above) is also returned as a ResearchGate listing, confirming it is a real, indexed publication (cross-check of source existence, not of content). — [ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390069463_F1tenth_Autonomous_Racing_With_Offline_Reinforcement_Learning_Methods)

### Inferences
- The team's plan already has a classical MPC/minimum-curvature reference for *evaluation*; these findings suggest that same reference (or a cheaper waypoint/Pure-Pursuit-style controller) could double as a *demonstration source* for offline pretraining (CQL/IQL/decision-transformer/diffusion-policy) or as a residual-RL base controller (RLPP-style), either of which is a concrete, low-risk technique this specific literature shows works for bootstrapping generalization, and which the current plan does not mention using the MPC reference for anything beyond final comparison.

## Q7: Observation/representation-learning tricks for generalization, independent of RL algorithm

### Takeaway
Two concrete, algorithm-independent representation levers appear repeatedly: (1) curvature-profile/lookahead-based track encodings (geometric, low-dimensional, directly analogous to what a min-curvature/MPC reference already computes) and (2) learned latent encodings of track/road geometry (VAE-encoded road masks, recurrent-VAE task-manifolds) — with at least one explicit caveat that raw curvature features "fall short" on pronounced curves and can hurt generalization if not designed carefully. Graph-based track/scene representations exist prominently for multi-agent traffic/behavior tasks but no racing-track-geometry-specific graph representation was confirmed in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- A search-tool synthesis (paraphrasing unnamed source material, not independently traced to one specific citable paper) states: "the choice of track representation... influences learning efficiency and generalization," identifying two common approaches — "geometric representation using curvature profiles, track widths, or range measurements" — and that "the agent needs its current position and information on the upcoming curvatures to plan on the track," with "features that enforce generalization to unseen tracks and waypoints" being an explicit design goal in at least one (unnamed) paper. This is consistent with, but not more specific than, standard F1TENTH/racing RL practice; **the specific paper(s) behind this synthesis could not be identified/traced**, so treat the general claim as plausible-but-unattributed. — synthesized characterization (search tool output), not directly traced to a titled paper
- The same synthesis notes an important **caveat/negative result**: "road curvature features often fall short in effectively representing curves, and in environments with pronounced curves, the learned policy tends to struggle with generalization" — again unattributed to a specific paper in what was fetched, but directly relevant as a risk the team should be aware of if they lean heavily on curvature-lookahead features (which the assignment's key questions specifically call out as a candidate technique).
- CLUTR (arXiv:2210.10243, Q3) is the clearest citable example of learned latent *task* representation (via a recurrent VAE over randomly generated tasks) driving generalization gains (10.6x on CarRacing vs. PAIRED) — this is a representation-learning trick that is explicitly algorithm-independent (used to drive curriculum generation, not the policy's own observations) but demonstrates the general principle that learned latent structure over the space of tracks/tasks materially helps zero-shot generalization. — [arXiv:2210.10243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10243)
- The Learn-to-Race-winning latent-planning paper (arXiv:2207.01275, Q1) is a citable example of learned latent *observation* encoding (VAE over U-Net road-segmentation masks) enabling strong cross-track performance (105 km/h known vs. 73 km/h unknown track, zero off-road violations) — i.e., compressing a vision-based road mask into a compact latent space before any planning/control step. — [arXiv:2207.01275](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.01275)
- InDRiVE (arXiv:2512.18850, Q1) demonstrates that the *pretraining objective* for a latent world-model representation (reward-free, disagreement-driven exploration) itself affects downstream zero-shot robustness under distribution shift (unseen towns/routes in CARLA), independent of which actor-critic algorithm consumes the resulting latents. — [arXiv:2512.18850](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.18850)
- Graph-neural-network representations were found prominently for **multi-vehicle/traffic-interaction** scenarios (e.g., arXiv:2006.12576, "Graph Neural Networks and RL for Behavior Generation in Semantic Environments," and a PMC survey on "Graph RL-Based Decision-Making for Connected and Autonomous Vehicles") where GNNs' permutation-invariance to a variable number/order of surrounding vehicles is the key motivating property — but **no paper was found applying a graph representation specifically to single-agent track/curvature geometry for zero-shot racing generalization**; this looks like an open technique gap in the racing-specific literature (as opposed to the general driving/traffic literature, where it is well established). — [arXiv:2006.12576](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.12576) (multi-agent traffic context, not track-geometry encoding); [PMC survey](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10575438/)

### Inferences
- Given the negative result about raw curvature features struggling on pronounced curves, a small team should treat "curvature-profile lookahead" (one of the techniques explicitly named in the assignment) as a reasonable-but-not-guaranteed baseline representation, and should consider it as a candidate for augmentation with (or replacement by) a learned latent encoding (VAE-style, per arXiv:2207.01275, or task-manifold-style, per CLUTR) rather than assuming hand-designed curvature/width/range features alone will generalize best.
- A graph-based *track-geometry* representation (as opposed to graph-based multi-vehicle representations, which are well established) appears to be a genuinely under-explored technique in this specific sub-literature — potentially a novel angle for the team if their Dreamer-based agent is meant to be the "novel contribution," since no directly competing prior work was found.

### Gaps
- The general claim about curvature-profile representations and their generalization limits could not be traced to a specific, citable paper — it appears only as an unattributed search-tool synthesis. The report-writer/team should not cite this as a hard fact without further sourcing; it is included here as a signal worth independently verifying (e.g., by searching directly for "curvature representation generalization F1TENTH" or checking the F1TENTH survey arXiv:2402.18558 directly).
- No paper applying graph neural networks to single-track curvature/waypoint geometry (as opposed to multi-agent scenes) for racing generalization was found; absence of evidence is not evidence of absence given the tool-call budget used, but it was specifically searched for and not located.
