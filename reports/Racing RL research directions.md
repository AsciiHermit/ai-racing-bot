# Bootstrap the Dreamer agent before broadening scope

The research converges on a clear answer: **ai-racing-bot should stay in racing, not expand toward real-world autonomous driving or another domain, and the next four techniques to try are all cheap extensions of assets the project already owns** — the classical MPC/minimum-curvature reference, the modular `Track`/`VehiclePhysics` protocols that can expose privileged state during training, and the Phase 6 evaluation harness. In priority order: (1) use the MPC reference as a demonstration/reward-shaping teacher rather than only a scoring baseline, (2) add an RMA-style privileged-context adaptation module on top of the Dreamer agent, (3) replace plain domain randomization with a cheap regret-proxy curriculum before considering full adversarial UED, and (4) adopt `rliable`-style interval statistics and a three-tier (default/interpolation/extrapolation) held-out set. Each is evidenced in adjacent robotics/RL literature and, for #2 specifically, a 2025 paper already fuses exactly this idea into a Dreamer-style racing agent. On scope, the evidence is unusually one-sided: no paper bridges racing RL to real-world open-road generalization, the resourcing gap is roughly four orders of magnitude (fleet-mile datasets and multi-GPU clusters versus a single repo), and the structural mismatch (no traffic, no long-tail, single dense reward versus multi-objective safety) means techniques wouldn't transfer even if resources existed. Broadening within racing (multi-agent tactics, strategy) is real and cheap architecturally, but the project's own phased plan already defers it correctly — do not pull it forward. The one credible adjacent domain, if the team ever wants to generalize the *contribution* rather than the *vehicle*, is terrain-randomized legged/ground-robot locomotion or drone racing — both share the identical procedural-environment-plus-swappable-physics structure — but that is a future, separate-project decision, not a near-term move.

## Bootstrapping from the MPC reference costs nothing and is the highest-confidence next step

The single most actionable, lowest-risk addition is one the plan already has the ingredients for: using the Phase 4 classical minimum-curvature/MPC solver not just as an evaluation reference but as an automated teacher. A 2026 loco-manipulation paper generated demonstration data from a sampling-based MPC expert at "one million samples per hour" via GPU-parallel simulation, seeded a TD3 replay buffer with 50% expert transitions, and phased them out on a curriculum as the agent's own success rate rose; the resulting policies **substantially outperformed the MPC teacher itself** — completion speed improved more than 50% on some tasks and variance dropped 11–45% — while a control run trained on sparse rewards with no expert data "completely fail[ed]" ([arXiv:2608.12063](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.12063)). This generalizes directly: the racing project's own per-track minimum-curvature solution can seed offline demonstrations, or act as a potential-based reward-shaping signal (a formulation that provably preserves the optimal policy set — [PBRS overview](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/potential-based-reward-shaping)) — and because it is procedurally regenerable for any spline track, it scales with the project's track generator in a way hand-authored demonstrations never could. Direct racing evidence supports the same move from a different angle: when online RL "consistently fail[ed] to complete laps" on F1TENTH's Austria track, switching to offline learning from a cheap waypoint-based suboptimal controller's rollouts fixed it and enabled explicit zero-shot-transfer testing ([arXiv:2408.04198](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.04198)); and RLPP, the best-quantified racing result in this whole research pass, layers an RL residual directly on top of a classical Pure Pursuit controller rather than replacing it, reporting **+6.37% lap time, a >52% gap-to-SOTA closure, and an 8x reduction in sim-to-real gap** versus a non-residual RL baseline on real F1TENTH hardware, with code open-sourced ([arXiv:2501.17311](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.17311)). None of this requires new infrastructure beyond wiring the existing MPC output into the replay buffer or reward function — it is a rerouting of a Phase 4 artifact, not a new phase.

## A privileged-context adaptation module is the direct upgrade path for the Dreamer agent

The second-priority technique is the one most tightly matched to the project's own novel contribution. The clearest transferable pattern from legged-robot sim-to-real research is **RMA (Rapid Motor Adaptation)**: train a base policy that sees privileged information unavailable at deployment (true terrain friction, true payload), then train a small online module to infer a compressed version of that privileged context from a short history of ordinary observations. On a real A1 quadruped, RMA "consistently outperformed baseline methods (fixed policies, domain randomization, system identification, AWR)" across success rate, time-to-failure, and distance covered on unseen terrain and payloads up to 12 kg ([arXiv:2107.04034](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.04034)). The mapping onto ai-racing-bot is exact: the privileged information is future track curvature and surface friction, the base policy is the Dreamer agent, and the adaptation module infers a latent track-context embedding online from recent sensor/state history — the project's `Track`/`VehiclePhysics` protocols already expose the ground-truth parameters needed to do this during training, so no new simulator infrastructure is required, only a second small network. Notably, this is not a hypothetical port: "Context-Aware Model-Based Reinforcement Learning for Autonomous Racing" (Moustafa & Dusparic, Oct. 2025) does precisely this — fuses meta-RL-style context inference into a Dreamer-style world model so the policy conditions on inferred track/friction/weather context, benchmarked directly against Dreamer, PPO, SAC, and TD-MPC ([arXiv:2510.11501](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11501)). Its numeric results were not machine-extractable in this research pass, so treat the paper as a design template and a verification target rather than a settled result — but it confirms the team would be replicating and extending a live research direction, not inventing an unproven one. If the adaptation-module engineering proves harder than expected, the fallback with real evidence is continual learning: sequentially pretraining SAC across several tracks then fine-tuning on a held-out one, explicitly evaluated zero-shot and few-shot on real RoboRacer hardware ([arXiv:2607.24320](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24320)) — a strictly simpler engineering lift than either RMA or classical meta-RL (PEARL/RL²), for which no racing-specific implementation was found at all.

## Curriculum design and evaluation rigor are the cheapest levers, and both beat plain domain randomization as currently scoped

Two adjustments cost almost nothing relative to their evidenced payoff, and both should be layered onto whatever agent is running rather than sequenced as separate phases. First, plain domain randomization of curvature/width — the current plan — is precisely the design that a 2023 zero-shot-generalization survey warns against: "taking a purely procedural content generation approach to benchmark design is not conducive to progress" unless the *training* distribution has genuine structural diversity, not just scalar-parameter variation ([arXiv:2111.09794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09794)). The best-evidenced fix, on the exact CarRacing testbed closest to this project's own environment, is CLUTR: a recurrent VAE learns a latent task manifold over randomly generated tracks, and a teacher generates curricula by maximizing minimax regret over that manifold, producing a **10.6x improvement in zero-shot generalization over PAIRED on CarRacing while using 500x fewer environment interactions** ([arXiv:2210.10243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10243)). Full adversarial UED (PAIRED/ACCEL-style) is real but expensive — it requires training a second adversary/generator agent plus a regret estimator, and the field is still actively stabilizing it (a 2023 paper is literally titled "Stabilizing Unsupervised Environment Design with a Learned Adversary," [arXiv:2308.10797](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.10797)) — a fair estimate for a small team is weeks, not days, of added engineering. A pragmatic middle ground with no adversarial infrastructure at all: track each agent's per-track-instance regret during ordinary domain-randomized training and oversample the tracks it currently performs worst on, capturing much of UED's curriculum-shaping spirit as a simple prioritized-replay heuristic. The drone-racing literature independently validates the same direction in a 3D racing-shaped task — combining a physically-informed procedural generator with learning-progress-based task switching reported a **7.4x improvement in generalization** over prior environment-shaping techniques, validated zero-shot on unseen real-world tracks ([arXiv:2606.27348](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27348v1)). Second, on evaluation: Agarwal et al.'s NeurIPS 2021 Outstanding Paper showed that point-estimate comparisons across a handful of seeds routinely produce conclusions that don't survive proper statistical treatment, and its `rliable` library operationalizes interval estimates and the interquartile mean specifically for small-seed-budget settings ([arXiv:2108.13264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264)) — directly applicable to the project's planned 3–5-seed Phase 6 comparisons. Just as important, a 2025 critique found that on some existing RL benchmarks a model trained directly on the "held-out" split scores the same as one trained properly — the held-out set silently failed to test anything ([arXiv:2510.10541](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10541)) — which argues for adopting Cobbe et al.'s default/interpolation/extrapolation tiering (train range, in-range random tracks, and genuinely out-of-range curvature/topology) rather than a single held-out bucket, so the generalization-gap claim in Phase 6 can't be accused of the same flaw.

## Real-world driving is not a small step away — it is a different project

The case against expanding scope toward real-world-style autonomous driving is not a matter of taste; it is a matter of evidence that the two research communities do not currently connect. A dedicated search for any paper using a racing environment — F1TENTH, CARLA racing scenarios, Gran Turismo — as a deliberate stepping-stone toward techniques later validated on real-world urban driving data (nuPlan, nuScenes, WOMD) found none; even Sony AI's own follow-up on GT Sophy states plainly that its inference "relies on global features... easily accessible in simulators but... challenging to obtain in real-world scenarios," naming cross-situation generalization "a remaining frontier" rather than a solved bridge ([arXiv:2504.09021](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.09021)). The resourcing gap compounds this: nuPlan alone contains roughly 500 million images and 1,500 hours of driving, Waymo's long-tail dataset required mining 6.4 million miles of fleet logs to surface 4,021 usable segments, and even mid-tier academic AD models train on 8–64 GPUs — a scale structurally incompatible with a small open-source team, whose only realistic entry point into this literature at all would be adopting an existing simulator like CARLA wholesale, which the source material itself frames as "essentially starting a new, largely unrelated project" rather than an extension of the current codebase. And the mismatch is not just resourcing but structure: real-world AD generalization research has spent 2025–2026 explicitly redesigning its benchmarks (Fail2Drive, Shift & Drift, BehaviorBench) around dense pedestrian/agent interaction and rare, safety-critical long-tail events — reporting, for instance, a 22.8% average success-rate drop under distribution shift on paired in-distribution/shifted route twins ([arXiv:2604.08535](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08535)) — precisely the two axes a closed-track, no-traffic racing simulator cannot represent by construction, no matter how sophisticated its tire model becomes. Set against this, the competitive-landscape research is reassuring rather than alarming about staying put: the one benchmark that scored cross-track generalization exactly the way this project intends (Learn-to-Race 2022, which produced a concrete ~39% speed drop on its held-out track, 92.53 vs. 152.09 km/h) has shown no confirmed continuation since 2022, and neither F1TENTH/RoboRacer nor MicroRacer score held-out track geometry as their primary metric — meaning the specific niche this project targets is open, not crowded, and there is no competitive pressure forcing a pivot.

## Broadening within racing is cheap and already scheduled — the mistake would be doing it early

Multi-agent tactics and race strategy are the one expansion direction with real architectural cheapness: they add new agent- and reward-level logic on top of the existing `VehiclePhysics`/`Track`/`Sensor` protocols without touching any of the three core interfaces, and GT Sophy is a citable precedent that this layer is a distinct, tractable research contribution once single-agent performance is solid ([*Nature* 602:223–228, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04357-7)). The Problem Definition document already reserves this correctly as a later chapter, and the research confirms that decision rather than overturning it — pulling it forward before Phase 6/7 land would compete directly with the dependency chain the team has already committed to, and would also dilute the one property the project's own architecture is best positioned to exploit: independently swappable track, physics, and sensor variables that keep a generalization claim from being confounded, a design logic that gets structurally harder to preserve as more uncontrolled factors (other agents' policies, right-of-way rules) enter the system. If the team eventually wants to generalize the *type* of contribution to a different embodiment rather than expand racing itself, the evidence points to two credible candidates and away from others: terrain-randomized legged locomotion (ETH Zurich's `legged_gym` pattern of procedurally varied terrain plus a swappable actuator/friction model plus sensor noise, mapping almost one-to-one onto the current protocol split) and drone racing (which already uses this project's exact vocabulary — procedural obstacle courses, zero-shot generalization to unseen environments, domain randomization plus curriculum). Both are mature, actively-published communities whose techniques (RMA, curriculum-over-generator-parameters) are the same ones recommended above, meaning the project doesn't need to choose between "stay narrow" and "learn from adjacent domains" — it should mine those domains for technique, exactly as this report recommends, while keeping racing as the evaluated domain.

## Conclusion

The convergent lesson across all four research threads is that this project's highest-leverage moves are not a new algorithm family or a new domain but better use of assets it is already building: routing the Phase 4 MPC solver into training as a teacher rather than only a scorer, exposing the privileged state its own modular architecture already has behind an RMA-style adaptation module, and tightening its Phase 6 statistics before the benchmark ships. Each of these is individually cheap, individually evidenced outside racing (and, for the adaptation-module idea, already being tried inside it), and — critically — each strengthens the exact generalization claim the Problem Definition document exists to make, rather than pulling the team toward a different claim entirely. The scope question resolves the same way: the literature shows no bridge, no comparable resourcing, and no structural overlap between closed-track racing RL and open-road real-world driving, so treating racing as a stepping-stone toward the latter would be a claim the field's own evidence does not support — while the fact that no one has "solved out" the lightweight cross-track generalization niche since Learn-to-Race went quiet in 2022 means there is no urgency to leave it either.
